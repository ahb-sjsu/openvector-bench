# g5 has a knob after all, and it is the one that was held fixed

**Exploratory, not a registered round.** No admission claim, seal untouched.
Measured 2026-08-10. Driver `harness/rc1/g5_probe.py`; record `results/g5.json`.
Follows `R38`, which it corrects. Reference: Blum, Hopcroft & Kannan,
*Foundations of Data Science*, §2.8 p.28.

## The claim being corrected

`R38` reported that g5 relative contrast "responds to nothing tried" — 2.670,
2.671, 2.672, 2.674 across a 2x range of `eff_rank` — and concluded that "a
quantity that responds to no available knob is not a tuning problem". That
conclusion was wrong, and the reason is instructive.

## What the book supplies

§2.8 derives, for two spherical Gaussians of dimension `d` with centres
separated by Δ:

* two points from the **same** cluster: `|x − y| ≈ sqrt(2d)`
* two points from **different** clusters: `|x − y| ≈ sqrt(Δ² + 2d)`

g5 is `mean random-pair distance / median 10-NN distance`, which is that ratio:

```
g5 ≈ sqrt(1 + Δ² / 2d)
```

Inverting: real's 1.369 implies `Δ²/2d = 0.874`; the generator's 2.67 implies
6.13. The centres were roughly **seven times too separated relative to the
within-article spread**.

Crucially, the governing quantity is the within-cluster **variance**, not its
dimension. In the builder the within-article displacement is
`inv = fil_scale / sqrt(fil_dim)`, so its norm is ≈ `fil_scale` regardless of
`fil_dim`. `R37` and `R38` varied `fil_dim` (16→30), `d_glob` (90→400) and the
linear-to-per-cluster change — and held `fil_scale` at 0.45 throughout.

**g5 was constant because the only quantity that controls it was never varied.**

**g5 moves from 2.658 to 1.348 on one knob**, passing through real's 1.369 near
`fil_scale` ≈ 1.37. The book's prediction is confirmed both in direction and
roughly in magnitude.

## Two of the three named defects share this knob

`eff_rank` rises with `fil_scale` too — 75.7 → 284.9 — crossing real's 182.3
near `fil_scale` ≈ 1.05. So g5 and `eff_rank`, two of the three defects `R37`
named, are governed by the same parameter and are close to simultaneously
satisfiable in the range 1.0–1.4.

**This also corrects `R38`'s tension result a second time.** `R38` found rank and
ramp in opposition because it moved rank with `d_glob`, which drove the b=100
ratio from 5.25 to 10.63. `fil_scale` moves rank over a *wider* range (75.7 →
284.9 against 111 → 227) while the ratio degrades only 5.44 → 6.56. The
opposition was a property of the knob, not of the family — the same conclusion
`R39` reached by a different route.

## The cost, which is now the binding constraint

**g6 hubness explodes: 1.586 → 3.754 → 18.920 → 21.004** against real's 1.696.
At the `fil_scale` that fixes g5 and `eff_rank`, hubness is an order of magnitude
too high, and g6 is **mandatory**.

The tension has therefore moved rather than disappeared: it is now
(g5, eff_rank) against g6, on `fil_scale`. As articles spread, they overlap and
the neighbour graph concentrates on a few rows.

Separately, `w_mean` behaves as expected and cheaply: at 0.5 the random-pair
cosine rises 0.007 → 0.111 against real's 0.2279, with no cost to g5, `eff_rank`
or the ramp. Real's anisotropy has to be built in — §2.4.2 and Fig. 2.5(a) say
high-dimensional points are near-orthogonal by default, so a cosine of 0.2279
will not emerge on its own. A value near 1.0 is indicated and was not tried.

## What this changes

* `R38`'s "nothing moves g5" is **withdrawn**. The knob existed; the sweep did
  not include it.
* Two of three named defects are now jointly reachable.
* The binding constraint is g6 hubness, which no round has yet attacked.
* `R38`'s rank-versus-ramp opposition is confirmed as an artifact of choosing
  `d_glob`, for the second time and by an independent route.

## What is not established

* Whether hubness can be controlled at large `fil_scale` — for instance by
  varying article sizes, which have been uniform at 23 throughout, or by the
  Zipf occupancy mechanism that `PARAMS` already exposes for the original
  family. `size_spread` has been 0 in every round of this arc.
* The b=100 ratio is 5.4–6.6 across this sweep against 4.050, so the ramp is not
  simultaneously satisfied here; `R36`'s 3.900 came from the linear build at
  `fil_scale` 0.45.
* No arm has been measured on the full §3b ladder or the s(k) curves; only the
  b=100 ratio was carried through.
